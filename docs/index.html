The order and the plan

Each mission opens with a five-paragraph order beside the map: Orientation, Situation (SALUTE and DRAW-D), Mission, Execution, Admin & Logistics, Command & Signal. In Execution, place control measures on the map (CP, OBJ, SBF, ASLT POS, PL, CCP) and write the scheme of maneuver as lines: a trigger (at step-off, after or with the line above, on a signal, at a time, on contact), a unit, a task and a place. Draft a plan gives you a hasty attack to start from. The clock runs while you plan, at 4× speed, and the field is frozen: no one moves or fires until you step off. The enemy uses the time all the same: a defender digs in, and an enemy on the march is placed as far along its route at step-off as it would have walked. Use a third of the time available and leave the rest to the fire teams. Press Step off to execute. The Tracker shows each line; fire signals there. A line halted short by contact waits for you to press Resume. The plan is graded at step-off, and the grade sets the squad's confidence: steadiness under fire, staying power, and how fast it answers the radio. After step-off your orders go by radio and take a few seconds to be acknowledged. The debrief grades the mission on plan, result, casualties and time.

Mouse

With a mouse, the left button selects and the right button acts. Left-click a man to select him, again for his team, again for the whole unit; left-click open ground to select nothing. Right-click the map for the action menu at that spot: move, rush or crawl there, bound, assault, suppress, fire 40mm, throw a grenade, or watch that way; right-click an enemy to engage him. Shift and right-click moves there at once. Hold the right button and drag to move there and face the way you drag. An order button (Walk, Assault, Suppress and the rest) arms that order: the next left click says where. While planning, right-click opens the plan menu. With a unit selected, the point tools also plan its moves: pick the squad (or a team), choose CP, and click checkpoint after checkpoint; each becomes its next step, marching from the last checkpoint to the next. RP, ASLT POS, FP, SBF, OBJ and TRP do the same with their own task, and a route drawn with RTE becomes that unit's route. With nothing selected the tools only draw. Each step shows a ghost of every team where it will stand when it arrives, with a dot for each man (fainter at earlier checkpoints). Q and E turn the selected unit on its own: a fire team (or each of several) about its own middle, the whole squad about the squad's; while planning they turn the unit's ghost. Right-click one of your units or a ghost for its menu: Face a direction (then click the way to face), formation, interval, and in the fight posture, fire control and halt, take cover, fall back. Where two or more teams go to the same point, a larger squad ghost (SQD) stands at their middle: click it to select them all there (the whole squad when it is all of them), drag it to move them together, and hold it with Q or E to pivot them. Drag a team's ghost to give it its own spot at that checkpoint; while holding it, Q and E pivot all the teams at that step about their middle, turning their formations with them, and they face that way on arrival (on a move, a bound, an assault, a fighting position or a support by fire position: a bound leads each team to its own spot, an assault sends each team through its own point, a fighting position lines each team up across the sector at its spot, a support by fire position sets each team at its spot before it opens fire, and on a suppress line each team's crosshair is its aim point on the target, so the squad's fire can be spread across a tree line or a row of buildings). The ghosts stay on the map after step-off: the steps still to come, and where each team moving on its own orders is going. Drag one to change it: a step still to come keeps its new spot, a step under way sends the team there at once, and a team's own destination becomes a new move order by radio. Suppression shows as each team's beaten zone, an amber ellipse along its line of fire (30 by 10 m to begin with) where its fire falls; click one to select it, then drag it to move it, drag an end handle to lengthen or turn it, or a side handle to widen it, while planning or in the fight. Team spots work for every plan step, from the map menu or the tools, for the whole squad, one team or several teams picked together (Shift-click, or double-click a team button for all but it): several teams on one point stand side by side, each with its own spot to drag, and teams sent along a route together split to their own spots at its end; the spot is kept relative to the checkpoint, so dragging the checkpoint moves the whole squad's spots with it. Whole squad (key 0) selects every team at once. The Execution tab lists every control measure (points, lines, areas): click one to find it on the map, Edit to change its kind or name, ✕ to delete it. On the map, click a line anywhere along it or an area anywhere inside to edit it, or drag it by the middle to move all of it. Drag one of your units with the left button to send it.

Selecting

Tap a Marine to select that Marine. Tap again to select the fire team, and again for the whole squad. The Squad and fire team buttons above the orders do the same. Keys: 1 2 3 fire teams, 0 squad, Esc cancel.

Moving

While planning, each planned move shows as a dashed blue arrow, and a dashed ghost of the unit stands where its last move ends. Each fire team gets its own ghost. Tap a ghost to pick that team there, Shift-click to add more, or double-click to pick every team at that spot; then tap the map to plan the next step. Drag a ghost to give its team its own spot at a checkpoint. Tap a control measure to change it into another kind (a checkpoint into an assault position, say), rename it, open it in the order or delete it; drag it to move it. While planning, nothing moves: select a unit and tap the map to open a menu that sets a checkpoint, a bound, a route, a fighting position, a support-by-fire position, an assault position, an objective or a target reference point at that spot and adds the line to the plan. Press on one of your pieces (finger or left button) and drag it: let go to open the plan menu at that spot while planning, or to send it there after step-off. After step-off, orders go by radio: tap the ground to move in the chosen mode (Walk unless you pick Rush or Crawl); tap twice quickly to rush. Hold the right button where the unit should go and drag toward the front it should face: let go and it moves there and turns to face that way. Double-click a fire team's button to select every other team instead. The squad leader moves with 1st Fire Team, but can still be selected alone, and the other teams move without him. Closer and Wider (keys Z and X) set the interval between the men of the selected team or squad, from half to three times the standard 7 m; in the fight they spread out or close up where they are. W, A, S and D shift the selected unit forward, left, back and right while it keeps its front; Q and E turn it. A ghost shows where it will end up, the order goes out when you stop pressing, and once there the men take up whatever move they were on. Right-click also moves. Teams and the squad move in their formation. A walking team that takes fire, or sees a new enemy within 250 m, halts on its own, gets down and returns fire; rush, assault or bound to keep moving under fire. Rushing is fast but tiring and easy to spot. Crawling is slow but men can crawl while pinned.

Fire and maneuver

Suppress puts fire on an area, seen enemy or not. Bound sends the squad forward one fire team at a time while the others cover it (a fire team bounds in buddy pairs). Assault walks forward firing and throwing grenades. Tap a visible enemy to make the selection engage that target.

Reading the pieces

Blue pieces are your Marines; red pieces are enemy you can see. A selected piece is lifted off the map in a gold halo. The shaded cone shows where a man is looking: he sweeps it left and right, wide and slow when calm, tighter and quicker when the enemy is near, fast and jerky under fire, and holds it on his target while firing. He is far more likely to spot an enemy inside his cone. The numeral beside him is his fire team. Cones toggles them (key V). Chits stacked on a piece show posture (STD, KNL, PRN), suppression (SUP, or PIN when pinned; a pinned man moves only by crawling), a wound (WIA) and no ammunition (AMO). A slash through a piece is a casualty; an X is killed. A red symbol in a dashed frame is where an enemy was last seen.

Overlay

Orders are drawn as on an acetate overlay: blue for friendly, red for enemy, black for targets. Blue lines with arrowheads are routes (long dashes for a rush, dots for a crawl); a heavy arrow with a solid head is an assault. OBJ marks the objective of a bounding drill. Dashed blue lines run from the shooters to a suppression target, a black cross in a circle. 40MM marks a grenade launcher target.

Scenarios, eras and weapons

New mission offers a scenario: today's generic enemy, or a force from history at its historical strength: a Wampanoag war party of about 200 at Swansea in 1675; a company of the 33rd Foot with Lord Cornwallis outside New York in 1779; a German Stoßtrupp and machine gun teams in Lorraine in 1917; 60 légionnaires of the 1er REP in the Algerian djebel in April 1961; Soviet motor rifles, naval infantry, paratroopers and spetsnaz from 1972 to 1987; an NVA platoon and Viet Cong guerrillas in Quang Nam in 1970; armed attachés at Les Cayes in 1994; PLA marines on a Spratly island; a Russian squad in a Zaporizhzhia tree line. Each says whether it is historical, a historical setting, historical fiction or alternate history. Custom battle lets you pick everything yourself: the enemy (any of them, or today's generic enemy) and its strength, from a fire team to a force of 200, your own force, the mission and the ground, so Viet Cong can fight in the Montana snow or Soviet paratroopers on the California coast. Your force is the era's own (Plymouth militia, Continental light infantry, Marines of 1918, 1970, 1983 or 1994, an ALN section, mujahideen) or any other, today's Marines included. Each force fights its own way: redcoats form line, hold their fire, give a volley at close range and charge with the bayonet; the war party fights from cover in loose order, works round the flanks and closes with the club when you waver; storm troops follow their machine gun's fire in with grenades and spades; a Soviet platoon attacks on line; guerrillas hold their fire to close range, hug you, and slip away; a gang fires wildly and scatters. Weapons follow their real range and power: hit chances fall off with range from each weapon's own figure, and wounds go with the round (an M16A4 hit is far more often fatal than a .22 from a Ruger 10/22; a .75 musket ball worst of all). Muzzle-loaders fire once and take 15 to 30 seconds to reload, and their black-powder smoke gives the shooter away; bows are nearly silent. Men within arm's reach fight hand to hand with bayonet, club, hatchet, sword or spade.

Map key

A squad fights on a 2 by 2 kilometer sheet of four 1-kilometer grid squares, starting 1 to 1.4 km from the enemy, beyond spotting range; larger units will get larger sheets. The sheet is in standard US military map colors. White is open ground. Light green with tufts is tall grass, which hides a man lying prone. Green is woods and hedgerows; rows of small green circles are orchards, green row lines are vineyards. Blue lines are streams, which run in shallow gullies that give cover; blue areas are ponds and cannot be crossed. A broad blue band is a river: it can be crossed only at a bridge (two black lines across the road) or at a ford, marked FORD in a dashed blue circle, where men wade slowly in the open. Pale blue-green ground with small blue tufts is swamp: very slow going and no cover, but it hides a man. Red-brown lines are contours (5 or 10 meters apart, as the margin states), with a heavier, numbered index contour every fifth line; numbers with a dot are hilltop heights. Black blocks are buildings. A black line with ticks is a stone wall, which gives hard cover to a kneeling or prone man. The red road is a paved road; the dashed double lines are dirt tracks. Each mission is set in one of six regions, chosen in the New mission dialog or at random: rolling farmland; the Southern California coast (Pacific Ocean on the west edge, sand beach, chaparral); South Texas brush country (mesquite thickets, pasture, stock tanks); North Carolina pine woods and blackwater swamp; the Arizona desert and mountains (open creosote flats, rock outcrops, dry washes shown as dashed blue lines); and the Montana mountains in winter (deep snow, lodgepole pine, frozen ponds). The ground changes the fight: snow, sand, chaparral and rock are slow; brush and grass hide a man lying down; rock gives cover; open desert gives long fields of fire. The 3D button (key T) turns the map into a sand table: the same sheet laid over the ground in 3D, heights exaggerated, with every man standing on it as a peg under his billet symbol (gray when hit; a red wire sphere marks where an enemy was last seen), and key terrain and the reported enemy position on flagpoles. Planning works there too: tap with a tool to place a control measure, drag one to move it, tap the ground with a unit selected for the plan menu. Drag to move, right-drag or two fingers to turn and tilt, wheel or pinch to zoom; the buttons beside it turn, tilt and zoom on a phone. Taps give the same orders as on the flat map. Fog of war: ground your squad can see now is clear; ground it has seen before is faded; ground it has never seen is faded more (key F toggles the wash). Point at the map to read the 8-digit grid reference. Measure (key M) lays a tape between two taps and marks the distance, grid bearing and mils on the map; tap a label to remove it. Point at a formation button to see the selected team or squad ghosted in that formation. Zoomed out, your men gather into one counter per fire team, then one for the squad, in the standard unit symbol: a rectangle with an infantry X for friendly, a diamond for enemy.

Camera

Drag to pan. Use the wheel or pinch to zoom. P pauses; you can give orders while paused. Hold Space (on the flat map or the 3D sand table) to see where every unit is going: a ghost of each team at its destination, its route, how it moves, how far and how long it will take; units holding are ringed where they stand.
